# Offer Engine

**Personalised offers for e-commerce customers on Telegram, always within the business's own rules.**

An online store sends offers to many customers, but not every offer is worth sending: a discount that wins back a
customer who is about to leave earns money; the same discount to someone who would have bought anyway only costs
margin. Offer Engine decides **which offer each customer should get, and when**, and delivers it through the
store's Telegram bot.

Built for the J-PAL South Asia Punjab Jobs AI Bootcamp, Hackathon 2.

- **Rules first.** The business sets the limits: discount caps per category (always below its margin), a budget,
  how often a customer may hear from you, quiet hours. Nothing breaks them.
- **AI inside the limits.** An LLM (Groq) picks among the offers that already passed the rules and writes a
  friendly message. A validator re-checks every AI answer; the exact terms are always generated by code.
- **Measured honestly.** A control group of similar customers is held back on purpose, so results show what
  offers really *added*, with uncertainty ranges.
- **For any business.** Sign up, upload your data (or try demo data), connect your own Telegram bot, and run
  offers and deals from a plain-language portal.
- **Synthetic data only.** The demo store's 5,000 customers, a year of orders, carts, browsing and reward points
  are generated, with realistic Indian shopping patterns.

> Status: feature-complete for the hackathon. Next: hosting on Render (deliberately the last step).

## Quick start

1. Python 3.11+, and a free Postgres database from [Neon](https://neon.tech).
   ```bash
   pip install -r requirements.txt
   ```
2. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` and fill in `DATABASE_URL`, `SESSION_SECRET`, `TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY`,
   `ADMIN_PASSWORD` (long random strings). Optional: `GROQ_API_KEY` (keep `LLM_ENABLED=false` unless testing)
   and `SHOPKART_BOT_TOKEN` for the demo store's bot.
3. Create the tables and the demo store's data:
   ```bash
   alembic upgrade head
   python scripts/seed.py --tenant shopkart
   python scripts/build_features.py --tenant shopkart
   python scripts/create_account.py --tenant shopkart --email you@example.com
   ```
4. Run the web app and, in a second terminal, the bots:
   ```bash
   uvicorn app.main:app --reload
   ```
   ```bash
   python scripts/run_bot.py
   ```
5. Open http://127.0.0.1:8000 to log in as ShopKart, or create a new business at `/app/register`.

## The portal (for businesses)

| Page | What you can do |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Today's numbers, a setup checklist, active deals, and the latest activity: who got which offer and why, or which rule held one back. |
| **Customers** | Search and filter by group or "on Telegram". Each customer's page explains their behaviour in plain words, recommends the best offer the rules allow (with alternatives), and lets you **create a custom offer** that is checked against every rule before it can be sent. |
| **Deals** | Describe a deal once ("Diwali Deal: up to 15% off", for chosen groups, on each customer's favourite category, the whole store or chosen categories, with a budget). Preview who gets what, why others are left out and the most it can cost, then launch. Each customer gets a version that fits them; percentages are lowered to each category's limit. Download coupons as CSV for customers you reach by email, SMS or your app. |
| **Activity** | Every decision, filterable by result. |
| **Results** | What offers and deals added compared with the control group, by deal, offer and customer group, plus Telegram claims. |
| **Data** | Upload customers, products, orders and order items as CSV (plus optional carts and points), using the templates provided. Files are checked first and every problem is listed by file and row; nothing changes until all pass. Or generate demo data in one click. |
| **Settings** | Connect your Telegram bot (paste the BotFather token; it's verified, encrypted and started automatically). Edit your brand voice, category margins (discount limits follow), offer rules and customer groups, all in plain language with change history. |

Customers on Telegram get offers instantly (or at 9:00 if it's quiet hours); everyone else gets a saved coupon
code. Offers you send by hand skip only the "how often" limits; every discount, budget and margin rule still
applies, and so does a customer's opt-out. Each business only ever sees its own data.

## The Telegram bot

Share `t.me/<your bot>`. In Telegram: **/start** → pick a kind of shopper (a demo persona from the business's
customers) → **Get my offer** → **Claim** or **Not now**. Also `/profile`, `/switch`, `/stop`, `/resume`, `/help`.
Every rule applies here too: a customer who just got an offer is told when the next one is allowed.

## Rules a marketing team can read

Each business's rules start from sensible defaults and are edited in Settings (the demo store's originals are in
`tenants/shopkart/rules.yaml`, every setting commented):

- **Limits**: discount cap per category (kept below its margin), most one offer can be worth, a daily budget,
  2 offers per customer per week, 48 hours between offers, no repeat within 14 days, quiet hours 21:00–09:00,
  no money-off offers to customers who would probably buy anyway or who return a lot, and a minimum expected
  profit per offer.
- **Automatic offers**: cart comeback, win-back, birthday treat, second-order welcome, points expiring, deal of
  the day, loyalty bonus points, free delivery, each with who may get it and a priority.

Every save is checked: a 12% cap on 8%-margin electronics, or ₹100 off a ₹999 order under a 10% store-wide cap,
is refused with a clear message instead of reaching customers.

## AI, safely

When switched on, Groq (`openai/gpt-oss-120b`) gets the customer's facts as data and **only the offers that
already passed the rules**. It picks one and writes a short intro. The validator then rejects an answer that
picks anything else, or whose text states numbers, prices, percentages, deadlines, extra promises, links, HTML or
another category, including answers that "obey" instructions hidden in customer data. On rejection, failure, or
when switched off, the rules' own top offer and a template are used, and the reason is recorded.
To stay inside the free tier: off by default, per-minute and per-day caps (counted in the database), answers
cached per customer per day, and tests and simulations never call it.

## Measuring what offers add

A stable 10% of customers form a **control group**: they don't get automatic offers or deals, and the offer
they *would* have got is recorded. Comparing similar customers with and without an offer gives the uplift in
purchases (with a 95% range), the extra profit after discounts and the rupees returned per rupee of discount.
For demo data, customers respond according to a hidden persona the engine never sees, so the engine's estimates
are tested against a ground truth.

## Realistic synthetic data

- **Hidden personas** (loyal, regular, bargain hunter, occasional, new, at risk, dormant, window shopper) drive
  purchase frequency, price sensitivity, coupon and points use, and cart abandonment.
- **Indian shopping calendar**: Big Festive Sale, Diwali build-up and post-Diwali lull, Republic/Independence Day
  sales, end-of-season fashion sale, monsoon dip, weekend and payday lifts.
- **Lifecycle and economics**: welcome bursts for new sign-ups, fading at-risk customers; fashion returns ~22% vs
  grocery ~1%; COD commoner in smaller cities; points earned, redeemed and expiring.
- Checked against known patterns: top 20% of customers ≈ 65–70% of revenue, ≈ 70% cart abandonment, festive spikes
  (`python scripts/seed.py --tenant shopkart --dry-run` prints the report).

Customer groups are worked out from behaviour alone. They match the hidden personas about half the time, which
is honest: a customer who started drifting last month still looks like a regular.

## Developer console and scripts

`/admin` (HTTP basic auth, `ADMIN_USER` / `ADMIN_PASSWORD`) shows every business: overview, results with
uncertainty bars, groups, decision log, a "what would they get now" customer page, the rules editor, and actions
(simulate campaigns, send a live campaign, fire live events).

```bash
python scripts/preview_offers.py --tenant shopkart [--customer 17]          # what the rules allow, explained
python scripts/try_agent.py --tenant shopkart --customer 17 --llm           # one real AI call
python scripts/simulate_campaign.py --tenant shopkart --days 7 --reset      # simulated campaigns + results
python scripts/live_trigger.py event --tenant shopkart --chat <chat id> --kind cart_abandoned
```

## Tests

```bash
pytest
```

180+ tests on a temporary SQLite database (never Neon, Telegram or Groq). They cover realism of the data,
every rule limit (plus a randomized check that no offer ever breaks one), the AI validator including prompt
injection, measurement, the bot service, and the portal, including that one business can never see or change
another's data.
